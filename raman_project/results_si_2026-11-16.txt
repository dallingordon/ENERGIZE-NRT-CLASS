SIMULATED Si RAMAN vs. CONTRASTIVE MATCHING NETWORK -- first results (2026-11-16)
=================================================================================

QUESTION
  Can a Raman spectrum simulated from the crystal structure alone (DFT, no experimental input)
  be identified by a contrastive spectra-matching network trained on experimental RRUFF data?
  Network: Li, Schmidt & Alstrom, "Raman spectrum matching with contrastive representation
  learning", Analyst 147 (2022) 2238. Code + trained weights from the authors (unmodified).
  Si is the test case before quartz: one Raman-active mode, and it is in the RRUFF library.

WHAT WE RAN
  Simulation  VASP, PBE. Relax -> Gamma phonons (DFPT) -> Raman activities from finite-difference
              dielectric tensors (+/-0.01 A along each optical mode) -> Lorentzian-broadened spectrum.
              Result: triply degenerate optical mode at 502.45 cm-1 (expt ~520.7; PBE runs soft).
  Inputs      Converted to the model's grid with the repo's own interpolation + min-max norm.
                si_sim x1.0    DFT spectrum as computed           (peak 502 cm-1)
                si_sim x1.036  DFT frequencies scaled by 1.036    (peak 521 cm-1)
                silicon_514/532/780  experimental RRUFF Si at three laser wavelengths (controls;
                                     peaks 519 / 521 / 517 cm-1). These may themselves be in the
                                     reference library, so they test the plumbing, not the model.
  Setup       "excellent_unoriented" model + reference library: 3554 RRUFF spectra, 1310 minerals,
              76-1403 cm-1. 6 independently trained models ("repeats": separate encoder + comparison
              head each, different random seeds). Every input is scored against every reference with
              every model; nothing is aggregated until post-processing.
  Score       Unnormalized similarity logit from the network's comparison head (higher = more
              similar). Per-mineral score = best score over that mineral's reference spectra.
              Ensemble = plain mean of the 6 models' scores (authors' method).

TAKEAWAYS
  1. Pipeline is sound: all experimental Si controls -> Silicon, rank 1, every model, big margins.
  2. With the known PBE frequency softening corrected (x1.036), the purely simulated spectrum is
     identified as Silicon by all 6 models, with a margin (+6.7) comparable to the experimental
     514 nm control (+5.5).
  3. Uncorrected (18 cm-1 low), identification fails: Si score collapses from ~6.5 to ~-1, every
     candidate scores negative (no confident match), Diamond comes out on top. The network is very
     sensitive to absolute peak position; DFT frequency error matters more than spectral shape here.
  4. The models agree completely on in-distribution inputs and disagree on the out-of-distribution
     one (x1.0: ranks 1-4). Model disagreement looks like a useful "don't trust this match" signal.

RUNNER-UPS (are they reasonable?)
  Romeite / Fluorcalcioromeite (consistently #2-#3 for the Si-like inputs): Sb-antimonate oxides of
    the romeite group (pyrochlore supergroup), whose spectrum is dominated by one Sb-O stretching
    band near 510 cm-1 -- i.e. another single strong peak next to Si's 520. Very reasonable; the two
    names are the same mineral group with near-identical spectra, so effectively one answer.
  Diamond (top match for unscaled sim): also a single sharp line, but at ~1332 cm-1. Suggests that
    when position doesn't match anything, the network falls back on "one sharp peak" shape.

CAVEATS / OPEN QUESTIONS
  - Ensemble = mean of raw logits, but the models' score scales differ (repeat_2 scores everything
    ~40% higher), so the mean is dominated by the widest-scaled model. Rank-averaging or per-model
    z-scoring would be fairer. Per-model results above are the safer thing to read.
  - Reference library = authors' training split only (their held-out test split not included).
  - The x1.036 scale factor is a generic PBE correction picked to land on 521 cm-1; with one peak,
    any correction that hits ~520 will look good. Quartz (many peaks) is the real test.
  - Only the excellent_unoriented setup run so far; the "raw" setup (1190 refs / 487 minerals,
    different preprocessing) is cached and ready.
